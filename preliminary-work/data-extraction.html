Data Extraction [Experiment Part II]

3 archival document images · 5 models · 3 prompt conditions · 10 runs for each condition

Structured data extraction against a 9-field schema (name, titles, dates, place of stay, family, origin) on three pages chosen to span content density: 18 travelers (page 28, an Ebrei page), 37 travelers (page 36, the densest), and 10 travelers (page 39). Each (model, page) combination is run under three conditions — naive (prompt only; N), guided (with expert-authored domain knowledge; A), and guided + transcription (guidance plus a pre-computed transcription; B) — for 10 runs each.

Method & Scoring how the evaluation works and why

  • The Evaluation Method
    What the method measures and how it scores model outputs in four different ways (count, align, measure transcription, and characterize what is left over), in explanation of how the method addresses the two separate questions: how many travelers were found, and how faithfully their names were read.
  • Name Similarity Measures Compared
    Comparison of the different methods explored (Ratcliff-Obershelp, CER, normalized edit distance, token F1 and Jaro-Winkler) to provide evidence for the choice of using Ratcliff to decide identity and CER to measure transcription.
  • Name Matching & Field Conflation
    What the name score is, and where a model puts the right value in the wrong field (a title or place of origin written into the name, which is correct in its own column and breaks the match from the other).
  • Raw vs Normalized
    What normalization and the equivalence rules are actually worth, per page and model in each condition.
  • Field Value Variations
    Raw results from each model and condition used to define both normalization rules and equivalence criteria.
